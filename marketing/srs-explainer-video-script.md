# "Why You Forget Words" — Lexivo SRS Explainer Video Script

**Length:** ~8 min 50 s · **Scenes:** 35 · **Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps
**Narration:** English and Uzbek versions are given for every scene. Pick one per video. Visuals are the same for both.
**Structure:**
1. The problem (0:00–0:50)
2. Why we forget: Ebbinghaus (0:50–2:00)
3. Discovery 1, spacing (2:00–3:30)
4. Discovery 2, testing yourself (3:30–4:24)

## 0. GLOBAL STYLE GUIDE — read before generating any scene

### Art style (apply to EVERY scene)
- Flat 2D vector animation, modern explainer style. Clean shapes, rounded corners, soft drop shadows, subtle paper-grain texture.
- Friendly, warm, optimistic. Smooth, simple character motion; nothing jerky.
- **No** photorealism, **no** 3D rendering, **no** anime style, **no** real people's faces.
- Backgrounds are simple and uncluttered. Exactly one clear focal point per shot.

### Colour palette
| Use | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Background (parts 1–6) | Warm cream | `#FFF8EC` |
| Main accent (parts 1–6) | Teal | `#14B8A6` |
| Forgetting / warning / wilting | Orange | `#F59E0B` |
| Remembered / correct / healthy | Green | `#22C55E` |
| Text, outlines | Dark navy | `#1E1B4B` |
| History scenes (1880s, 1960s) | Sepia tint over the palette | — |
| **Lexivo brand (part 7 ONLY)** | **Purple** | **`#6C63FF`** |
| Lexivo light background (part 7 ONLY) | Lavender | `#EEF2FF` |

### Main character: MADINA (keep identical in every scene)
> **MADINA:** a 20-year-old Uzbek university student, flat 2D vector style. Medium-brown skin, dark brown hair in a high ponytail, round friendly face, large dark eyes, thin eyebrows that make her expressions easy to read. She wears a **mustard-yellow hoodie** (`#EAB308`), dark blue jeans, white sneakers, and a small teal backpack. No glasses. Never in purple until part 7, where she may hold a phone with a purple app on it.

**For tools that generate one clip at a time:** wherever a scene says **[MADINA]**, paste the full description above into the prompt. Clip tools do not remember earlier scenes, and without it she will look different every time.

### Visual metaphor used through the whole video: the WORD GARDEN
- Every word Madina learns is a **small glowing seed → sapling** in a garden that stands for her memory.
- **Forgetting** = the sapling wilts and turns orange.
- **Reviewing** = watering the sapling with a small watering can. It turns green and stands up.
- **Long-term memory** = a full tree with deep roots, visible underground in cross-section.
- Same garden, same look, every time it appears.

### On-screen text: IMPORTANT
AI video tools usually draw text wrong (misspelled, warped letters). **Do not let the video tool generate any readable text.** Generate clean visuals with empty space, then add every "On-screen text" line below **afterwards in a video editor** (CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci). The same goes for graphs: generate the curve or bars, then add the labels in the editor.

### Audio
- Background music: soft, warm acoustic/lo-fi with light piano, low under the voice. It changes mood at Scene 22 (more tense) and at Scene 27 (bright, uplifting).
- Narration voice: calm, warm, clear, not salesy. About 140–150 words per minute.
- Sound effects are listed per scene. Keep them subtle.

### Scene card format
Every scene below has: **Narration EN**, **Narration UZ**, **Visual** (what is on screen), **Motion** (what moves), **Camera**, **On-screen text** (added in the editor), **Sound**, **Transition**.

## PART 1 — THE PROBLEM

### Scene 1 · 0:00–0:12 (12 s) · "Madina learns words"
**Narration EN:** "This is Madina. Tonight she learned twenty new English words. She wrote them down, said them out loud, and felt sure: 'I've got these.'"
**Narration UZ:** "Bu — Madina. Bugun kechqurun u yigirmata yangi inglizcha so'z o'rgandi. Ularni daftariga yozdi, ovoz chiqarib takrorladi va o'ziga ishonch bilan aytdi: «Bularni endi bilaman.»"
**Visual:** Night. A cosy bedroom desk with a warm yellow desk lamp, a cup of tea, and an open notebook. [MADINA] sits at the desk writing in the notebook with a blue pen. A window behind her shows a dark blue night sky with a few stars.
**Motion:** Madina writes, looks up, mouths a word, nods to herself, closes the notebook, and gives a small confident smile and thumbs-up.
**Camera:** Starts on a medium shot from the side, slowly pushes in to her confident face.
**On-screen text:** "reluctant — istamay, ikkilanib" (shown as if written on the notebook page, in a handwriting font), then "20 new words ✓"
**Sound:** Pen scratching on paper, soft lamp hum, gentle music starts.
**Transition:** Simple wipe to the next scene, with a calendar page flipping by.

### Scene 2 · 0:12–0:26 (14 s) · "A week later: gone"
**Narration EN:** "A week later, she's reading an article — and there it is: 'reluctant.' She knows she learned it. She remembers the page, even the colour of the pen. But the meaning? Gone."
**Narration UZ:** "Bir haftadan keyin u maqola o'qiyapti — va mana u: «reluctant». U bu so'zni o'rganganini biladi. Hatto qaysi sahifaga yozganini, ruchkaning rangini ham eslaydi. Lekin ma'nosi-chi? Esidan butunlay chiqib ketgan."
**Visual:** Daytime, inside a city bus. [MADINA] sits by the window holding a smartphone with a plain white reading screen (generic grey text lines, no app branding). One word on the screen is highlighted in orange.
**Motion:** She reads calmly, then stops and freezes. A thought bubble appears above her head showing the notebook page from Scene 1, but the space where the meaning was written is blank and fades away like smoke. A big orange question mark pops up next to her head. Her face goes from calm to confused.
**Camera:** Over-the-shoulder shot of the phone, then a cut to a close-up of her confused face.
**On-screen text:** "7 days later" (top corner), "reluctant = ???"
**Sound:** Bus engine rumble, a soft "record scratch" or a "blip" at the freeze, then a puzzled "hmm" sound.
**Transition:** Her confused face shrinks into the centre of the next scene.

### Scene 3 · 0:26–0:38 (12 s) · "Every learner knows this"
**Narration EN:** "Sound familiar? Almost every language learner knows this feeling. You put in the hours, the notebook fills up — but the words just don't stay."
**Narration UZ:** "Tanish holatmi? Deyarli har bir til o'rganuvchi bu tuyg'uni biladi. Soatlab vaqt sarflaysiz, daftar to'lib boradi — lekin so'zlar xotirada qolmaydi."
**Visual:** A grid of 6 small square panels, each showing a different young learner (various ages, genders, skin tones, all flat 2D) staring at a notebook or flashcard with the same confused face and an orange question mark. Madina is in the centre panel.
**Motion:** The panels pop in one by one. Then a thick notebook in the foreground flips through its pages quickly, full of handwritten words, and the words on the pages fade out one by one like disappearing ink.
**Camera:** Static wide shot, then a slow zoom on the fading notebook.
**On-screen text:** none
**Sound:** Pages flipping fast, a soft "poof" each time a word fades.
**Transition:** The fading notebook pages blow away like leaves.

### Scene 4 · 0:38–0:50 (12 s) · "It's not you — it's the brain"
**Narration EN:** "Is it a bad memory? Not enough talent? No. This is how every human brain works. And scientists have been studying it for more than a hundred years. Let's look at what they found."
**Narration UZ:** "Bu yomon xotiradanmi? Iqtidor yetishmasligidanmi? Yo'q. Har bir inson miyasi shunday ishlaydi. Olimlar buni yuz yildan ortiq vaqtdan beri o'rganib kelishadi. Keling, ular nima topganini ko'raylik."
**Visual:** Cream background. Two labels, "Bad memory?" and "No talent?", appear on the left and get crossed out with a red X. On the right appears a friendly, simple cartoon brain (soft pink, smiling, with small eyes), and around it float old scientific notebooks and a stack of old books.
**Motion:** The labels pop in and get crossed out; the brain bounces in gently. The books orbit the brain slowly.
**Camera:** Static, centred.
**On-screen text:** "Bad memory? ✗", "No talent? ✗", then the title card: **"Why do we forget?"**
**Sound:** Two "buzzer" sounds for the crosses, then a soft "ding" for the brain.
**Transition:** Sepia colour washes over the screen from the edges, leading into the 1880s.

## PART 2 — WHY WE FORGET (Ebbinghaus)

### Scene 5 · 0:50–1:12 (22 s) · "Ebbinghaus experiments on himself"
**Narration EN:** "In the 1880s, a German psychologist named Hermann Ebbinghaus ran an unusual experiment — on himself. He memorised long lists of meaningless syllables, like 'DAX', 'BOK' and 'YAT', so that nothing he already knew could help him. Then he tested how much he remembered after twenty minutes, an hour, a day, a week."
**Narration UZ:** "1880-yillarda nemis psixologi Hermann Ebbinghaus g'ayrioddiy tajriba o'tkazdi — o'zining ustida. U «DAX», «BOK», «YAT» kabi ma'nosiz bo'g'inlardan iborat uzun ro'yxatlarni yodladi, shunda oldingi bilimlari unga yordam bera olmasdi. Keyin yigirma daqiqadan, bir soatdan, bir kundan va bir haftadan so'ng qanchasini eslab qolganini tekshirdi."
**Visual:** Sepia-toned 19th-century study room: wooden desk, oil lamp, bookshelves, a pocket watch on the desk. A generic illustrated 19th-century scientist (NOT a portrait of the real man): a middle-aged man with a big full beard, round glasses, dark waistcoat and a white shirt. He holds small paper cards.
**Motion:** He flips cards one by one, reading them with concentration. Then four clock faces appear in a row above the desk (20 min, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week), lighting up one after another; each time one lights up, he scribbles a result in his notebook.
**Camera:** Medium shot, slow pan from the pocket watch to his face.
**On-screen text:** "Hermann Ebbinghaus, 1885" (lower-third label), card texts "DAX", "BOK", "YAT" (added in editor onto blank cards), clock labels "20 min · 1 hour · 1 day · 1 week"
**Sound:** Ticking pocket watch, card flicks, quill scratching. Music: soft, curious.
**Transition:** The page of his notebook zooms in and becomes the graph in Scene 6.

### Scene 6 · 1:12–1:28 (16 s) · "The forgetting curve"
**Narration EN:** "What he found is now called the forgetting curve. Memory drops fast at first — much of what he had learned was gone within the first day — and then it keeps sliding down, more slowly, over the days that follow."
**Narration UZ:** "U topgan narsa bugun «unutish egri chizig'i» deb ataladi. Xotira avvaliga juda tez pasayadi — o'rganganlarining katta qismi birinchi kunning o'zidayoq unutilgan — keyin esa keyingi kunlarda sekinroq, lekin to'xtovsiz pasayishda davom etadi."
**Visual:** A clean graph on old paper (keep a slight sepia tint). Vertical axis = "memory", horizontal axis = "time". A thick orange line starts high at the left and drops steeply, then flattens into a long, slow downward slope. Small floating word-cards sit on the curve and fade as the line drops.
**Motion:** The orange line draws itself from left to right: a steep plunge, then a slow glide. The word-cards above the line turn transparent one by one as the line passes under them.
**Camera:** Static, full-frame graph.
**On-screen text:** Axis labels "Memory" / "Time", a label at the steep drop: "Day 1: most of it fades", title "The Forgetting Curve"
**Sound:** A descending "whoosh" synced with the steep drop, then a low sustained tone.
**Transition:** The graph paper slides left to reveal Scene 7.

### Scene 7 · 1:28–1:48 (20 s) · "But relearning is faster"
**Narration EN:** "But he noticed something else. When he relearned a list he had 'forgotten', it took less time than the first time. The memory wasn't completely gone — it was just weak. And that is the key: forgetting is not the end of a word. It's a signal that the word needs one more push."
**Narration UZ:** "Ammo u yana bir narsani payqadi. «Unutilgan» ro'yxatni qayta yodlaganida, bu birinchi martadagidan kamroq vaqt oldi. Demak, xotira butunlay yo'qolmagan — shunchaki zaiflashgan edi. Mana asosiy nuqta: unutish — so'zning oxiri emas. Bu so'zga yana bir turtki kerakligining belgisi."
**Visual:** Split screen, sepia. LEFT: the same scientist learning the list, with a large stopwatch above him showing a long time (the stopwatch ring nearly full). RIGHT: him relearning the same list, with the stopwatch ring only partly full. Then a faint, dim word-card in the middle of the screen glows back to full brightness when tapped.
**Motion:** Both stopwatches run; the right one stops early. The scientist on the right raises an eyebrow and smiles. Then the dim card in the centre gets a gentle "push" (a little hand nudges it) and lights up.
**Camera:** Static split, then a zoom into the centre card.
**On-screen text:** LEFT "First time", RIGHT "Relearning: faster!", centre "Forgotten ≠ gone"
**Sound:** Two stopwatch ticks, a "click" when the right one stops, a soft sparkle when the card lights up.
**Transition:** Sepia fades out to full warm colour; the glowing card falls to the ground like a seed, leading into Scene 8.

### Scene 8 · 1:48–2:00 (12 s) · "Words are saplings" (garden metaphor starts)
**Narration EN:** "Think of every new word as a young sapling. Planted today, it's fragile. Leave it alone, and it wilts. So the question is: when should you water it — and how?"
**Narration UZ:** "Har bir yangi so'zni yosh nihol deb tasavvur qiling. Bugun ekilgan nihol hali nozik. Unga qaramasangiz, so'lib qoladi. Savol shuki: uni qachon va qanday sug'orish kerak?"
**Visual:** THE WORD GARDEN for the first time: a small, sunny garden bed with rich brown soil, on the cream background. [MADINA] kneels and plants a tiny glowing seed; it sprouts immediately into a small green sapling with two leaves. Next to it, another sapling (planted earlier) is drooping and turning orange.
**Motion:** Madina pats the soil. The new sapling sprouts. Time-lapse: the sun crosses the sky quickly, and the new sapling starts drooping as well. Madina looks at it, then at a watering can sitting nearby, and tilts her head, questioning.
**Camera:** Low, ground-level shot near the soil, then tilt up to Madina's thoughtful face.
**On-screen text:** "When? How?" (appears next to the watering can)
**Sound:** Soil patting, a small "pop" when the sprout appears, birdsong, a soft wilting "droop" sound.
**Transition:** Cut to the two-learner comparison.

## PART 3 — DISCOVERY 1: SPACING

### Scene 9 · 2:00–2:18 (18 s) · "Two learners, same time"
**Narration EN:** "Here's the first big discovery: spacing. Imagine two learners — the same twenty words, the same total study time. The first studies them all in one long session, the night before an exam. The second splits the same time into short sessions on different days."
**Narration UZ:** "Birinchi katta kashfiyot — oraliq bilan takrorlash. Ikki o'quvchini tasavvur qiling: so'zlar bir xil — yigirmata, o'qishga ketgan umumiy vaqt ham bir xil. Birinchisi hammasini bitta uzun mashg'ulotda o'rganadi — imtihondan oldingi kechada. Ikkinchisi xuddi shu vaqtni turli kunlarga bo'lingan qisqa mashg'ulotlarga taqsimlaydi."
**Visual:** Split screen with a vertical divider. LEFT: Learner A, a young man in a grey sweater, at a desk late at night surrounded by coffee cups, eyes tired, cramming. One huge block on a calendar strip below him (all hours on one day). RIGHT: Learner B, a young woman in a green cardigan, studying briefly and relaxed in daylight. A calendar strip below her shows small blocks spread across 4 different days.
**Motion:** LEFT: a clock spins fast; he yawns and rubs his eyes. RIGHT: the calendar days flip; she appears briefly each day, studies, smiles, and leaves.
**Camera:** Static split screen.
**On-screen text:** "Learner A: all at once", "Learner B: spread out", "Same words · Same total time"
**Sound:** LEFT: fast clock ticking, coffee slurp. RIGHT: gentle page turns, birds.
**Transition:** Both calendars slide down and two test papers slide up.

### Scene 10 · 2:18–2:32 (14 s) · "Spacing wins"
**Narration EN:** "Test them a few weeks later, and the second learner remembers far more. Pouring the whole bucket at once just floods the plant. A little water, spread out over time, lets the roots grow."
**Narration UZ:** "Bir necha haftadan keyin ularni tekshirsangiz, ikkinchi o'quvchi ancha ko'p narsani eslab qoladi. Bir chelak suvni birdaniga quyish nihoga faqat zarar qiladi. Oz-ozdan, vaqt bo'yicha taqsimlangan suv esa ildizlarning o'sishiga imkon beradi."
**Visual:** First: two bars side by side, a short orange bar for A and a tall green bar for B. Then the garden: on the left, Learner A dumps an entire big bucket of water onto a sapling at once; water floods over and the sapling falls flat. On the right, Learner B gives a sapling a small splash from a watering can on each of several days; the sapling grows taller each time, and a cross-section of the soil shows its roots growing deeper.
**Motion:** The bars grow upward. Then the flooding and the step-by-step growth play out side by side.
**Camera:** Static on the bars, then cut to a wide garden shot with an underground cross-section.
**On-screen text:** "A few weeks later…", bar labels "A" / "B"
**Sound:** A big water splash and gurgle (left), gentle sprinkles (right), a rising "growth" chime.
**Transition:** The roots of the right sapling extend downward and become a stack of papers in Scene 11.

### Scene 11 · 2:32–2:50 (18 s) · "300+ experiments agree"
**Narration EN:** "This is called the spacing effect, and it's one of the most tested ideas in all of psychology. In 2006, a team led by Nicholas Cepeda reviewed more than three hundred experiments on it. The result was clear: spaced practice beats cramming — again and again."
**Narration UZ:** "Bu «oraliq effekti» deb ataladi va u psixologiyada eng ko'p tekshirilgan g'oyalardan biri. 2006-yilda Nicholas Cepeda boshchiligidagi olimlar bu mavzudagi uch yuzdan ortiq tajribani tahlil qilishdi. Natija aniq edi: oraliq bilan takrorlash bir o'tirishda yodlashdan qayta-qayta ustun keldi."
**Visual:** A modern university office. A diverse group of 3 illustrated researchers (generic, not real portraits) stands around a very tall, wobbly stack of research papers. Each paper that flies onto the stack has a tiny green checkmark on it.
**Motion:** Papers fly in from off-screen and land on the stack quickly, faster and faster; a counter counts up to 300+. The researchers look at each other and nod.
**Camera:** Starts low, tilts up the tall stack of papers.
**On-screen text:** "The Spacing Effect", counter "300+ experiments", "Cepeda et al., 2006"
**Sound:** Paper flutter, rapid "tick-tick-tick" counter, a satisfying "ding" at 300+.
**Transition:** The stack topples gently and the papers spread into 10 cards for Scene 12.

### Scene 12 · 2:50–3:06 (16 s) · "Only 2 of 10 techniques get top marks"
**Narration EN:** "In 2013, John Dunlosky and colleagues compared ten popular study techniques — highlighting, re-reading, summarising, and more. Only two received their highest rating: spreading practice out over time, and testing yourself."
**Narration UZ:** "2013-yilda John Dunlosky va hamkasblari o'nta mashhur o'qish usulini solishtirishdi — matnni belgilash, qayta o'qish, qisqacha mazmun yozish va boshqalar. Faqat ikkitasi eng yuqori bahoni oldi: mashg'ulotlarni vaqt bo'yicha taqsimlash va o'zini o'zi tekshirish."
**Visual:** Ten simple cards in two rows of five, each with an icon: highlighter pen, open book (re-reading), notepad (summary), lightbulb, keyword key, image, etc. Two of the cards — one with a **calendar icon** and one with a **question-mark/quiz icon** — will light up.
**Motion:** All ten cards appear. Eight of them dim to grey and shrink slightly. The calendar card and the quiz card rise up, glow green, and get a gold star each.
**Camera:** Static, then a gentle push in on the two winning cards.
**On-screen text:** "10 techniques compared", winners labelled "Spaced practice ★" and "Testing yourself ★", "Dunlosky et al., 2013"
**Sound:** Eight soft "dim" sounds, then two bright "star" chimes.
**Transition:** The calendar card flips over and becomes a family photo for Scene 13.

### Scene 13 · 3:06–3:30 (24 s) · "The 9-year family study"
**Narration EN:** "And for vocabulary specifically, there's a remarkable study. Harry Bahrick and his family — four people — each learned three hundred foreign words and followed them for nine years. They practised with gaps of two, four, or eight weeks between sessions. The longest gaps felt harder at the time — but years later, those were the words they remembered best."
**Narration UZ:** "So'z yodlash bo'yicha esa ajoyib bir tadqiqot bor. Harry Bahrick va uning oilasi — to'rt kishi — har biri uch yuzta chet tili so'zini o'rganib, ularni to'qqiz yil davomida kuzatishdi. Ular mashg'ulotlar orasida ikki, to'rt yoki sakkiz haftalik tanaffus qilishdi. Eng uzun tanaffuslar o'sha paytda qiyinroq tuyuldi — lekin yillar o'tib, aynan o'sha so'zlar eng yaxshi esda qolgan edi."
**Visual:** A warm living room. A generic illustrated family of four adults (not real portraits) sits around a kitchen table with flashcard boxes. Behind them a wall calendar. Then three garden beds side by side, labelled by gap length: the "2 weeks" bed has small trees, the "4 weeks" bed has medium trees, and the "8 weeks" bed has the biggest, strongest trees with the deepest roots.
**Motion:** The family studies flashcards; the wall-calendar pages fly off rapidly (years passing), and the family's hair turns slightly grey to show time. Then cut to the three garden beds: the trees grow; the 8-week trees grow last but tallest. One family member wipes their brow ("it was hard") and then smiles at the big trees.
**Camera:** Medium shot of the family, then a wide shot of the three garden beds.
**On-screen text:** "Bahrick et al., 1993", "4 people · 300 words each · 9 years", bed labels "every 2 weeks", "every 4 weeks", "every 8 weeks", final line "Harder gaps → stronger memory"
**Sound:** Calendar pages flapping, a clock ticking faster, a warm "growth" swell.
**Transition:** Cut to a split screen of two learners (Scene 14).

## PART 4 — DISCOVERY 2: TEST YOURSELF

### Scene 14 · 3:30–3:42 (12 s) · "Looking vs. recalling"
**Narration EN:** "The second big discovery is about how you review. Looking at a word and its translation again feels productive. But trying to recall it — before you see the answer — is far more powerful."
**Narration UZ:** "Ikkinchi katta kashfiyot — qanday takrorlash haqida. So'zga va uning tarjimasiga qayta qarash foydali tuyuladi. Lekin javobni ko'rishdan oldin uni eslashga harakat qilish — ancha kuchliroq."
**Visual:** Split screen. LEFT: [MADINA] reading a notebook where both the English word and its translation are visible, nodding passively. RIGHT: [MADINA] holding a flashcard with only the English word visible, the back covered by her hand, eyes closed, concentrating hard. Then she flips the card and smiles.
**Motion:** LEFT: slow, sleepy nodding. RIGHT: a small "thinking" spark above her head, the card flips, a green check appears.
**Camera:** Static split.
**On-screen text:** LEFT "Re-reading", RIGHT "Recalling"
**Sound:** LEFT: a dull page turn. RIGHT: a "thinking" twinkle, a card flip, a bright "ding".
**Transition:** The right-hand flashcard zooms into the foreground and becomes a Swahili card for Scene 15.

### Scene 15 · 3:42–4:06 (24 s) · "The Swahili experiment"
**Narration EN:** "In 2008, Jeffrey Karpicke and Henry Roediger had students learn forty Swahili words. Some kept re-studying words they already knew. Others kept testing themselves on them. One week later, the students who kept testing remembered about eighty percent. The ones who only re-studied — around a third."
**Narration UZ:** "2008-yilda Jeffrey Karpicke va Henry Roediger talabalarga qirqta suahili so'zini o'rgatishdi. Ba'zilari allaqachon bilgan so'zlarini qayta-qayta o'qishda davom etishdi. Boshqalari esa o'zlarini shu so'zlar bo'yicha tekshirishda davom etishdi. Bir haftadan keyin o'zini tekshirganlar taxminan sakson foizini eslab qolgan edi. Faqat qayta o'qiganlar esa — taxminan uchdan birini."
**Visual:** A university lab room with a row of computers. Two groups of generic illustrated students: the LEFT group re-reads cards (relaxed, passive); the RIGHT group answers quiz cards (focused, writing answers). A card example floats in the middle: a sailing boat icon. Then two big bars appear: the left bar (re-study) about one-third full in orange, the right bar (testing) about 80% full in green.
**Motion:** The groups study. A "1 week later" calendar flip. The bars fill up from the bottom: the orange one stops low, the green one keeps climbing high.
**Camera:** Wide lab shot, then cut to the bar chart full-screen.
**On-screen text:** "Karpicke & Roediger, 2008", "40 Swahili words", example card "mashua = boat", "1 week later", bar labels "Re-studied: ~1/3" and "Tested: ~80%"
**Sound:** Keyboard clicks, a calendar flip, the bars rising with an ascending tone; a triumphant chime on the green bar.
**Transition:** The green bar turns into a water pipe going down into the garden soil (Scene 16).

### Scene 16 · 4:06–4:24 (18 s) · "Effort builds the path"
**Narration EN:** "Every time you dig a word out of your memory, the path to it gets stronger. That small moment of effort — 'wait, I know this…' — is exactly where learning happens. And even if you get it wrong and then see the answer, you'll remember it better than if you had never tried."
**Narration UZ:** "Har safar so'zni xotirangizdan «qazib» olganingizda, unga olib boradigan yo'l mustahkamlanadi. O'sha kichik harakat lahzasi — «to'xta, men buni bilaman-ku…» — aynan o'rganish sodir bo'ladigan joy. Hatto xato qilib, keyin javobni ko'rsangiz ham, umuman urinmagandagidan ko'ra yaxshiroq eslab qolasiz."
**Visual:** The WORD GARDEN in underground cross-section. A sapling's roots reach down, and a thin glowing path connects Madina's head (shown at the top edge, thinking) down to the sapling's roots. Each time she "recalls", the path gets thicker and brighter.
**Motion:** Three pulses of light travel down the path from her head to the roots; with each pulse the path thickens and the roots grow longer. On the third pulse, she frowns (wrong), a hint card appears, she says "ahh!", and the path still gets brighter.
**Camera:** Vertical cross-section, slow downward pan following the light.
**On-screen text:** "Recall = stronger path", "Even mistakes help"
**Sound:** Three soft "whoomp" pulses, rising pitch each time; a small "oops" sound, then "aha".
**Transition:** Pan back up to the surface and out to a long timeline (Scene 17).
